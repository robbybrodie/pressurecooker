The graph auto-pans and auto-scales as phase and household settings change. By default the 1990 peak aligns with the 2028 stress point. The blue line uses a real 1998 historical continuation point; the orange dashed branch splines directly from the 2028 stress point to the 2034 easing scenario. All circles sit on their lines.
Historical basis: the aggressive easing cycle began around the 1990 peak; 1992 shows much of that easing delivered, and 1998 is grounded in actual 1998 earnings, Sydney dwelling value and the 6.7% standard variable housing rate. Future basis: 2028 uses the RBA WPI/CPI path; 2034 uses explicit long-run wage/CPI assumptions and the user-selected easing reduction.
Data & modelling assumptions
1998: ABS full-time adult ordinary earnings: $773.20/week male, $646.90/week female; Sydney median owner-occupied dwelling value ≈ $251,000; RBA standard variable housing rate 6.7%. Historical childcare/ancillary household-cost scaling remains a proxy.
2028: nominal wages factor 1.0609 and CPI/cost factor 1.0506 versus 2026, based on the RBA August 2026 forecast path. The property value is CPI-indexed to hold the 2026 property constant in real terms; this is not a house-price forecast.
2034: wages grow 3.0% p.a. and CPI 2.5% p.a. after 2028. 2028 tax settings are held constant unless legislated changes are modelled later, so bracket creep can appear. Mortgage rate = 2028 stress rate minus the easing slider.
Sources: RBA August 2026 outlook · ABS 1998 earnings · ABS 1997–98 housing · RBA May 1998 housing rates.
